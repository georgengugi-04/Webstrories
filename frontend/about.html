Our Story

Built From Conversations in the Field, Not Just at a Desk

GreenTrack began in June 2026 as a student project — a collaboration between our team at KCA University and Jomo Kenyatta University of Agriculture and Technology (JKUAT), together with JHUB Africa.

Mission: Reconnect people with the origin of their food.

GreenTrack is a farm-to-table traceability platform — a record that starts the day a seed goes into the ground and doesn't stop until the plate reaches a table. For growers, it's a plot manager: GPS-pinned plots, irrigation logs, and photo-based pest diagnosis. For everyone downstream — a buyer in a market aisle, a chef checking a delivery, a diner scanning a table tag — it's meant to become a single QR code that answers questions about where their food actually came from.

We're not trying to replace the relationship between a grower and the people who eat what they grow. We're trying to give that relationship something it rarely has: a record neither side has to take entirely on faith.

Before writing a line of that record, we went and stood in it — visiting growers around Subukia, Kenya, to see how the app actually holds up on a real plot, in someone's hands. Read what came out of those visits in the Journal.

Our Standards

What "verified" is meant to mean on a GreenTrack tag

The intent behind a batch tag is that it only appears after four things happen in order: a plot is GPS-pinned, irrigation and any treatment are logged as they happen, a pre-harvest safety interval clears on its own countdown, and a harvest weight is recorded.

We hold the stories in our Journal to a similar bar: we don't publish an interview or a photograph as fieldwork unless it comes from a real visit we made, with the person's knowledge and, where documented, their written consent.
